# Judge Question Bank and Defense Preparation

Prepare concise, evidence-backed answers. The team should be able to explain its decisions without reading the proposal aloud.

## 1. Problem and brief

1. What exact problem from the competition brief are you solving?
2. Who experiences this problem most directly?
3. What evidence shows the problem is real and relevant?
4. Why did you choose this user segment rather than another?
5. What is outside your scope, and why?

## 2. Research

1. How did you recruit participants?
2. Why is the sample appropriate for exploratory research?
3. What is the most important insight from users?
4. Which finding changed your initial assumption?
5. What are the limitations of your research?
6. How did you avoid generalizing from a small sample?

## 3. UX and feature decisions

1. Why does each core feature exist?
2. Which feature addresses the most severe user pain point?
3. Can you trace this screen to a research finding?
4. What did you decide not to build, and why?
5. How do users recover from errors?
6. What would happen if the user does not complete the main task?

## 4. Accessibility and inclusion

1. Which accessibility needs did you design for?
2. Which needs did you actually test with users?
3. What accessibility checks were performed, and which were not?
4. Does the prototype demonstrate screen-reader behavior or only specify it?
5. How do you avoid disclosing sensitive information unnecessarily?
6. How would you validate the design with a user whose needs are not represented in your current research?

## 5. UI and interaction

1. Why did you choose this design system?
2. How does visual hierarchy support the core task?
3. What changed between low-fidelity and high-fidelity design?
4. Which interaction was hardest to make clear?
5. What states are simulated in the prototype?

## 6. Testing and iteration

1. Who participated in the usability test?
2. What was the exact task success definition?
3. What does the SUS score tell you—and what does it not tell you?
4. What was the most problematic task?
5. What design change followed the test?
6. Did you retest the change?
7. How would results differ for the primary target user group?

## 7. Innovation and competitors

1. What can users do with your solution that is meaningfully harder with existing alternatives?
2. How did you verify competitor features?
3. Is your differentiation a feature, a workflow, a service, or an outcome?
4. Why would users switch from current workarounds?
5. What is the strongest assumption in your innovation claim?

## 8. Impact and feasibility

1. Who operates the service behind the interface?
2. What data or partner is required?
3. What happens if that partner does not participate?
4. How will you verify data quality?
5. What would a pilot look like?
6. How would you measure impact after launch?
7. What is your biggest implementation risk?

## 9. Difficult but useful questions

1. What is the weakest part of your current evidence?
2. Which feature would you remove first if time were cut in half?
3. What assumption might invalidate the product concept?
4. Which user group is under-represented?
5. What did users dislike or fail to understand?
6. What does your best metric fail to prove?
7. What would make your solution unnecessary?
8. What evidence would convince you to change direction?

## Answer format

Use this compact structure:

- **Answer:** direct response in one sentence.
- **Evidence:** one relevant finding, screen, test, or source.
- **Boundary:** what is not yet validated or implemented.
- **Next step:** what the team would do next.
